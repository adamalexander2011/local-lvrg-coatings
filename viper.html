Floor systems

Four systems. One standard of prep.

Every job starts the same way: diamond grinders and dust extraction down to a clean, open profile. What changes is what goes on top of it.

Mechanically polished, densified, sealed

Your existing slab, ground through progressive diamond grits, chemically densified and burnished to the sheen you pick. No coating to chip, nothing to re-apply. The floor is the finish.

  • Retail floors, warehouses, showrooms, modern homes
  • Gloss levels from a soft satin to a full wet-look shine
  • Takes fork traffic, pallet jacks and grocery carts without complaint
  • Cleans with a neutral cleaner and an auto-scrubber — no wax, no strip-and-recoat

How it goes

What actually happens between the call and the floor.

01

We look at the slab

On site, in person, free. We check for moisture, previous coatings, cracks, spalling and how the slab was finished. Then you get a written scope and a fixed number — not a per-foot guess over the phone.

02

We grind it open

Diamond grinders with HEPA dust extraction take the surface down to a clean, open profile. Old paint, mastic and sealer come off. Cracks get chased and filled. This step is why the floor lasts.

03

We build the system

Primer, base, broadcast, topcoat — or grind, densify, polish. Applied at the mil thickness the manufacturer specifies, at the temperature and humidity the product actually wants.

04

We hand it back clean

Walk the floor with you, hand over the care sheet and the cure times, take the trash with us. You know exactly when you can walk on it and when you can park on it.

Straight answers

Questions people actually ask us.

My garage floor was painted years ago and it’s peeling. Can you go over it?

No — and anybody who says yes is about to sell you the same failure again. Paint and roll-on kits sit on top of the slab instead of bonding into it, so a new coating over them is only as strong as the layer underneath. We grind the old material off completely and start from clean concrete. It costs a little more up front and it’s the difference between a floor that lasts and one that flakes off with your tires next winter.

How long does a two-car garage take?

Most flake systems are a one or two day install. Day one is grinding, crack repair and the base coat with the flake broadcast. Day two is scraping the excess flake back and rolling the polyaspartic topcoat. You can usually walk on it that evening and park on it about 24 hours later.

Polished concrete on a larger commercial slab runs longer — typically two to four days depending on square footage and how many grit passes the finish calls for.

My slab has cracks. Is it a lost cause?

Almost never. Ordinary shrinkage cracks get chased out into a V, vacuumed and filled with a structural polyurea that cures harder than the concrete around it, then ground flush. They disappear under a flake system and read as a faint line under a clear polish.

What we’ll tell you honestly: an actively moving crack, a heaved slab, or one with real structural movement behind it needs that cause addressed first. We’ll say so at the estimate rather than coat over it and let you find out in the spring.

Can you install in the winter in Michigan?

Yes. Heated and enclosed spaces are no issue at all. For unheated garages and pole barns we bring temporary heat and use products formulated to cure at lower temperatures — polyaspartics in particular are far more forgiving in the cold than standard epoxy.

Winter is genuinely our easiest scheduling window. If you want the floor done before spring gets busy, this is when to call.

What does it cost?

We won’t give you a number over the phone, and you should be wary of anyone who will. Prep drives the price more than the coating does — a clean bare slab and a slab with three failed coatings on it are completely different jobs, and nobody can tell which one you have without standing on it.

So the visit is free, it takes about twenty minutes, and you leave with a fixed written price for your floor rather than a per-foot average that moves once we start grinding.

Will it yellow, and will hot tires pull it up?

The topcoat is what determines both. Straight epoxy will amber over time under UV and can be lifted by hot tire pickup. That’s exactly why we finish flake systems with a UV-stable polyaspartic instead — it holds its color and it doesn’t soften under a hot tire that’s just come off the highway.

How do I take care of it?

Sweep or dust mop it, and wash it with a neutral cleaner and warm water when it needs it. No wax, no strip-and-recoat, no sealing every year. Keep harsh acidic cleaners off it and put felt pads under anything heavy you plan to drag. That’s the entire maintenance program.
